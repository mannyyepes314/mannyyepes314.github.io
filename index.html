Manuel A. Yepes

I'm in my first year of law school at NYU Law. I spent the last two years finishing an MPhil in Economics at Oxford, where I thought about the categories we use and construct to describe Federal Reserve asset purchase programs (quantitative easing versus market functioning purchases). Before that, I studied Social Studies at Harvard College, where I wrote a thesis asking why a socialist party enacted free market reforms in 1980s Spain.

Questions I have

  • Can the U.S. (and do people think the U.S. will) pay back the national debt? Are U.S. government bonds overvalued? What's the difference between debt monetization and technical Treasury market management?
  • Who's deciding AI's aesthetic? Where did the 20th century science fiction conception of AI as the one great human achievement go, and why do we think of it as just an economically useful tool?
  • What will happen to universities in the future? Are there alternative social intellectual engines?
  • What, legally speaking, is the Federal Reserve? What does the political theory of central banking look like?
